## What the company does
We are a rooftop solar company in India. We sell, install and service on-grid and off-grid solar systems from 1 kW to 5 kW for homes and small commercial customers. We run more than 40 installation projects a month with more than 15 technicians, plus a steady stream of service calls on systems already installed. We carry each customer from the first enquiry through to the twenty-year panel warranty.

## Who does what in the company
Sales handles leads, customers, survey requests, quotations and capturing acceptance. The Operations Manager approves surveys, schedules and assigns work, resolves blocked work and maintains packages. Warehouse handles purchase receipts, stock, packing, scanning, dispatch and delivery confirmation. Technicians do surveys, installations, service visits and time entries on their own assignments. Support handles tickets, customer and asset lookup, warranty status and replacements. Finance handles invoices, payments, refunds, supplier and technician payables. The Director reads all operational and financial data and uses the dashboard. One person may hold several roles.

## The stages of a job from enquiry to after-sales
Enquiry, sales follow-up, qualification, site survey, survey approval, generated quotation, customer acceptance, order confirmation, payment clearance, stock reservation, packing and scan verification, dispatch, delivery confirmation (the warranty starts here), installation and testing, customer acceptance, balance settlement, project completion, then after-sales support. Payment, delivery, installation and support are tracked separately: a delivered order is not automatically installed, and an installed system is not automatically paid for.

## Handling an enquiry and qualifying a lead
Enquiries come from the public web form or staff enter them from a call or message. Capture the monthly bill and the monthly units in kWh as separate fields, and never work one out from the other. A lead moves New, Contacting, Qualified, Survey Scheduled, and can go to Lost from any stage with a reason. Before qualifying, Sales checks roof availability, consumption, grid connection suitability, what the customer wants and whether documents are complete. Every lead needs an owner and a next action with a due date. A matching phone or address is flagged as a possible duplicate for staff review; never discard or merge it silently.

## What a site survey must record
The technician records roof dimensions and usable area, orientation and tilt, shading, the cable route and measured distance, site access notes, grid connection details, photos, and a recommended system type and capacity. Off-grid surveys also record the critical loads and the backup duration asked for. A survey goes Draft, Submitted, then Approved, Rejected or Changes Requested. No customer-ready quotation can exist without an approved survey. An approved survey is never edited; a later change makes a new revision and forces a review of any quotation built on the old one.

## Quotation rules
The system generates the quotation from the approved survey, the reviewed package, measured quantities, the current price list and the subsidy estimate. Sales does not build a parts list by hand. A quotation is valid for 30 days from issue. If an input or a price is missing, generation stops and names what is missing. A revised quotation replaces the old one and needs fresh customer acceptance. An issued quotation keeps its prices even if the catalogue changes later.

## How a customer accepts a quotation
There are two ways, and both count the same. Supervised: staff show the quotation on a phone or tablet in a restricted view showing only the customer's document, and the customer signs on screen. Remote: staff send the PDF and a signing link to the customer's linked Telegram chat. A signing link works once, expires, and is tied to one chat and one quotation revision. A drawn signature is in-app evidence of acceptance, not an Aadhaar eSign or a certified digital signature.

## Payment terms and when goods can dispatch
Plans are advance or full payment. The default advance is 30% of the gross payable, and Sales may set it between 20% and 100%. The balance falls due when delivery completes, not when installation is accepted. Dispatch needs the agreed advance recorded as cleared (or the full amount on a full plan) and the stock ready. Staff record payments by hand; there is no payment gateway. Mistakes are fixed by a reversal or refund, never by editing a payment. Installation acceptance is not a payment gate.

## Stock, packing and scanning
Panels, inverters, batteries and charge controllers are serialized: each unit has an asset ID and usually a manufacturer serial. Cable and consumables are tracked by batch with a quantity; never invent a serial for a length of cable. Opening the packing list reserves the stock. Scanning rejects the wrong product, a unit allocated to another order, an already dispatched unit, a repeat scan, and closing packing with quantities missing. A manual fallback needs permission and a reason. We deliver complete orders only: a shortage blocks the order and raises a purchasing action instead of a partial dispatch.

## When the warranty starts
The warranty starts when delivery is completed, not at packing, dispatch or installation. Records are created automatically for every eligible unit. Our configured terms are 2 years for inverters and batteries and 20 years for panels, counted from delivery. These are our company policies, not universal manufacturer terms. The policy is frozen at delivery, so later policy changes do not alter existing cover. A replacement keeps the original's remaining cover unless the policy grants a fresh period.

## Doing an installation and getting sign-off
The crew fills in the installation form on site: actual start and finish time, confirmed system type and capacity, installed equipment by serial, checklist results, the power generation test result, captioned photos and notes. The customer then signs on the same screen. Materials must be recorded as delivered before the installation can be completed. If a customer refuses to accept, record the reason and the work stays open; refusal is a valid outcome, not an error.

## Handling a service complaint or ticket
A ticket moves New, Diagnosed, In Progress, Awaiting Customer Confirmation, Closed. A technician visit verifies the fault and records the recommendation before work goes ahead. Warranty eligibility is judged on when the complaint was recorded, so waiting for a technician never expires a valid claim. Covered work costs the customer nothing for parts or labour. Non-covered work needs an estimate and the customer's approval before work starts, and going beyond the approved scope needs fresh approval. A ticket closes only when the customer confirms, not when the technician says it is done. A closed ticket can be reopened with a reason.

## How subsidy appears on our quotations
A subsidy shows only where a rule exists for the site's state and the customer qualifies. Otherwise the quotation has no subsidy line. A residential scheme does not apply to commercial or off-grid systems unless its rule says so. An estimate is not an approval and not a payment. Never subtract an estimated subsidy from the invoice balance unless the payment arrangement really works that way. Capital subsidy and per-unit generation incentives are separate and are never added into one figure.

## How much cable we plan for a job
The quotation works out cable as surveyed route distance times 1.15 (the waste factor), plus 5 m (the termination buffer), rounded up to the catalogue's stocking unit. For example, a 20 m route gives 20 × 1.15 + 5 = 28 m. If the product is stocked in 5 m lengths, that rounds up to 30 m. Both figures are settings stored on each package version, so check the package if they have been changed.

## How we pick a battery for off-grid
Battery choice comes from the off-grid load and the backup hours recorded on the survey, never from panel capacity alone. Do not assume every inverter needs the same controller either: compatible parts come from the reviewed package templates.

## Where to find live company figures
The assistant has no live records. For a customer, order, payment, ticket or stock count, open that screen. The Director's dashboard shows enquiries, installations completed and outstanding, blocked work, collections, outstanding balances, purchases, supplier payables, technician hours, labour cost, open repairs, and which jobs made or lost money. Quoted margin, operational margin, cash collected and cash paid are four separate figures.
