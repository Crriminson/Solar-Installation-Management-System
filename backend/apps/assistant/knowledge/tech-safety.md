## Solar panels are always live in daylight
A panel produces voltage whenever light falls on it, and it cannot be switched off. A string can reach several hundred volts DC, even with the inverter off and the grid down. Treat every DC cable as live until you have measured it. DC shock and DC arcs are more dangerous than household AC because the arc does not self-extinguish.

## How to shut down a system safely before working on it
1. Switch the inverter off using its own switch or the manual's procedure.
2. Switch off the AC MCB in the ACDB.
3. Open the DC isolator.
4. Wait the time in the inverter manual for its capacitors to discharge.
5. Measure with a multimeter to prove the circuit is dead.
6. Only then disconnect connectors.
To restart, reverse the order: DC isolator on, then AC on, then inverter. Follow the inverter's manual if it differs. Remember the array side of the DC isolator stays live in daylight.

## Working safely at height on a roof
Use a harness tied to a sound anchor near any roof edge, and keep a second person on site. Secure the ladder at the top and keep three points of contact. Check sheet roofs for weak spots, and walk on purlins. Do not work on a wet or windy roof. Stop work if lightning is near. Keep tools tied or in a pouch and keep people clear of the area below. Wear rubber-soled shoes, and carry water on a hot day.

## Safe meter use and tools for solar work
Use a multimeter and clamp meter rated for DC at the string voltage (CAT III 1000 V is a common rating), not a basic household meter. Check the meter on a known source first. Use insulated tools, and wear insulated gloves for live checks. Keep hands dry. Never touch a broken panel without gloves, because a cracked panel can be live on its frame if the insulation has failed.

## What to do about a hot, burning or smoking connector or inverter
Keep people away. Switch off AC and the DC isolator if it is safe to reach them. Use a dry powder or CO2 extinguisher or sand on electrical fire, never water. Remember the array stays live, so tell the fire service it is a solar installation. Then call the Operations Manager and open a ticket with photos. The MSEDCL specification asks for portable extinguishers or sand buckets wherever norms require.
