## Never mix MC4 connector brands
Only mate connectors of the same brand and type, unless both makers document them as compatible. "MC4 compatible" connectors look alike, but pin shape and tolerances differ. A loose fit raises contact resistance, the joint heats up, and it can arc. Regulators in Australia have traced PV fires to mismatched DC connectors. If a panel's factory connector differs from yours, use a matching connector on your cable, not whatever is in the bag.
Source: https://worksafe.nt.gov.au/forms-and-resources/bulletins/electrical-safety-identifying-mismatched-d.c.-connectors-in-solar-pv-installations

## How to crimp an MC4 connector properly
Poor crimping is the most common cause of connector failure, because a bad crimp means high resistance and heat.
- Strip to the length the connector maker states, without nicking the strands.
- Use the crimp tool specified for that connector, not pliers.
- Check all strands are inside the pin.
- Push the pin into the body until it clicks, and tug to check it is locked.
- Tighten the gland to the maker's figure with the proper spanner.
Do not re-crimp a used pin; cut it off and start again. Torque and pull-test numbers differ by maker, so use that connector's instruction sheet.
Source: https://www.surgepv.com/blog/mc4-connector-crimping-best-practices

## Handling MC4 connectors safely
Never unplug an MC4 under load: DC arcs do not self-extinguish like AC. Switch the inverter off and open the DC isolator first. Check polarity before mating. Keep connectors off the roof surface and away from standing water, and clip them to the rail. Fit caps on unused connectors. On a service visit, look for browning, melting or a hot connector, and replace both halves as a pair.
