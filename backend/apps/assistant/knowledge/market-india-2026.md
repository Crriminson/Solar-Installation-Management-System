## India's total installed solar capacity
As of 31 August 2026, India had about 168 GW of solar: 123.99 GW ground-mounted, 32.59 GW grid-connected rooftop, 4.83 GW hybrid and 6.63 GW off-grid, according to MNRE figures. Nearly 34.45 GW was added in the first eight months of 2026.
Source: https://solarquarter.com/2026/09/11/indias-solar-capacity-crosses-168-gw-as-2026-installations-near-34-45-gw-by-august-2026/

## Rooftop solar growth in 2026
Mercom reports India added 6.6 GW of rooftop solar in the first half of 2026, up 136% from 2.8 GW in the first half of 2025. Q2 2026 alone was 3.8 GW, and rooftop was 33% of all solar added that quarter. Residential was 84% of Q2 rooftop additions, industrial 10%, commercial 5% and government 1%. JMK Research puts the half-year figure a little lower, at about 6.4 GW, so trackers differ.
Source: https://www.pv-magazine-india.com/2026/08/31/india-adds-6-6-gw-of-rooftop-solar-in-h1-2026/

## PM Surya Ghar progress so far
MNRE said on 4 August 2026 that the scheme had passed 50.06 lakh households, with a record of about 5.06 lakh added in July. The target is 1 crore households. Ember put residential capacity under the scheme at about 14.9 GW across 5 million households as of July 2026. Mercom says the pace depends on faster approvals, financing and delivery.
Source: https://swarajyamag.com/infrastructure/pm-surya-ghar-scheme-crosses-50-lakh-installations-as-july-delivers-record-506-lakh-additions

## ALMM rules and panel supply in 2026
ALMM is the government's Approved List of Models and Manufacturers. List-II, covering cells, applied from 1 June 2026, which has put pressure on domestic cell supply and pushed up DCR module prices. Mercom warned of a rush and price volatility in the second half of 2026. Qualifying net-metering and open access projects have a transition window until 31 December 2026. Give It Up residential consumers are exempt until 31 March 2027. Expect DCR panel prices to move, so confirm with Purchasing before committing to anything beyond the quotation's validity.
Source: https://www.pv-magazine-india.com/2026/08/31/india-adds-6-6-gw-of-rooftop-solar-in-h1-2026/

## Typical price of rooftop solar in India in 2026
Vendors quote about Rs 45,000 to 65,000 per kW installed in 2026, before subsidy, depending on panel brand, inverter and installer. These are vendor figures for the market, not our prices. Our quotation is generated from our own price list, so never quote this range as our rate.
Source: https://www.voltflow.net/blog/solar-panel-cost-india-2026

## GST on solar panels and inverters
The GST Council cut the rate on PV modules from 12% to 5% in the September 2025 revision. Sources disagree on inverters: some say 5% as part of a solar system and 18% when sold separately, and a Karnataka High Court ruling supported 5% for an older period. We have not seen the official notification. Our quotations use the GST rate set on each catalogue product, so ask Finance rather than quoting a rate from here.
Source: https://taiyangnews.info/markets/solar-modules-get-5-gst-rate-in-india

## Net metering rules are changing across states
Regulators are revising rooftop rules state by state. Delhi moved to a two-stage, fully online approval with tighter deadlines. Karnataka set Rs 2.34 per unit for non-DCR systems, and 75% of that tariff for virtual and group net metering. Maharashtra's regulator issued a draft proposing Rs 2.82 per unit for surplus rooftop export, and we could not confirm whether it became final. Always check the customer's own DISCOM rules.
Source: https://mercomindia.com/maharashtra-proposes-%e2%82%b92-82-kwh-tariff-for-surplus-rooftop-solar-power

## Net metering in Maharashtra with MSEDCL
Guides say MSEDCL accepts net metering applications from residential consumers with sanctioned loads up to 10 kW, and cite a 30-day window for net meter installation under MERC rules, but these are vendor guides. In February 2026, MSEDCL was reported to be limiting domestic rooftop capacity to average yearly usage, which an industry body disputes. How surplus units are credited is also unsettled. Check with the MSEDCL section office for a live case.
Source: https://www.thehitavada.com/Encyc/2026/2/16/msedcl-limits-solar-capacity-for-domestic-sector-to-average-1-yr-power-consumption.html
