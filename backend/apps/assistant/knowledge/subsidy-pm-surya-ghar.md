## What PM Surya Ghar is
PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana is the central government's residential rooftop solar scheme, launched in February 2024 and run by the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE). It gives homes a capital subsidy, called Central Financial Assistance (CFA), and aims to reach 1 crore (10 million) households. Applications go through the national portal at pmsuryaghar.gov.in.
Source: https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in

## Central subsidy amount for 1 kW, 2 kW, 3 kW and above
The central subsidy is Rs 30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and Rs 18,000 for the third kW. So 1 kW gets Rs 30,000, 2 kW gets Rs 60,000, and 3 kW gets Rs 78,000. Rs 78,000 is the maximum: a 4 kW or 5 kW system also gets Rs 78,000, not more. A part kW is pro rata: 2.5 kW gets 60,000 + 0.5 × 18,000 = Rs 69,000. We found no official revision of these slabs as of November 2026. Confirm on the portal before quoting.
Source: https://solar.delhi.gov.in/page/central-subsidy

## Who is eligible for PM Surya Ghar
The consumer must be an Indian citizen, own a house with a suitable roof, and hold a valid electricity connection in their name. Guides also say the applicant must not already have taken a solar subsidy, and that an existing solar system on the same connection rules them out. One guide says the spouse's name on the meter is also accepted; confirm with the DISCOM. It is a residential scheme, so ordinary commercial customers do not get this subsidy. These conditions come from secondary guides, so check the portal for a borderline case.
Source: https://www.drishtiias.com/important-government-schemes/ministry-of-new-and-renewable-energy/pm-surya-ghar-muft-bijli-yojana-1

## Income limits and group housing societies
One installer page mentions an income ceiling, but no other source does, so treat it as unconfirmed and do not tell a customer there is one. One vendor page lists Rs 18,000 per kW for group housing societies and residential welfare associations for common facilities, up to 500 kW at 3 kW per house. We could not confirm that from an official source. Check the portal before quoting a society.
Source: https://www.solarsquare.in/blog/types-of-government-solar-panel-subsidy-schemes-in-india/

## DCR panels and why the subsidy needs them
DCR means Domestic Content Requirement: the cells and modules are made in India. To claim the central subsidy, the installation must use DCR modules, and using non-DCR modules makes the installation ineligible. The utility side checks this through a DCR undertaking or certificate uploaded on the portal. Keep the module serials and the DCR paperwork for every subsidy job.
Source: https://www.cag.org.in/sites/default/files/database/2.%20PM%20Surya%20Ghar%20Scheme-%20A%20consumer%20manual%20%281%29.pdf

## The Give It Up option and non-DCR panels
Give It Up lets a household voluntarily forgo the central subsidy while still using the scheme's portal and net metering. An MNRE memorandum dated 8 June 2026 lets residential consumers who choose Give It Up use modules without a DCR certificate and exempts them from ALMM List-II, for projects commissioned up to 31 March 2027. An application without a DCR certificate cannot switch away from Give It Up once installation details are confirmed. An application with a DCR certificate can change its subsidy option until the redemption stage. Few customers choose this, because they give up as much as Rs 78,000.
Source: https://solarquarter.com/2026/06/09/mnre-grants-almm-list-ii-exemption-for-pm-surya-ghar-consumers-opting-give-it-up-scheme-until-march-31-2027/

## How to apply for PM Surya Ghar step by step
1. The consumer registers on pmsuryaghar.gov.in and selects their state and DISCOM.
2. The DISCOM checks feasibility. Guides give anything from 1 day to 15 working days.
3. The consumer picks a registered vendor and signs an agreement on size, cost and scope.
4. The vendor installs. Plant details and a net meter request go on the portal.
5. The DISCOM installs the net meter and inspects.
6. The DISCOM approves on the portal and a commissioning certificate is generated.
7. The consumer submits bank details and a cancelled cheque.
8. The subsidy is credited to the bank account.
Source: https://www.jbvnl.co.in/SOLAR/SOP%20JBVNL%20Surya%20Ghar_Final_18122024.docx

## Documents needed for the subsidy
For the consumer: electricity bill, identity proof, and bank details with a cancelled cheque. After installation the portal needs the inspection report, the DCR undertaking, a geo-tagged photo of the rooftop plant and the meter installation report, as listed in the Jharkhand utility's procedure. Our own quotation rule also lists the electricity bill plus identity and eligibility documents. Exact uploads can vary by DISCOM.
Source: https://www.jbvnl.co.in/SOLAR/SOP%20JBVNL%20Surya%20Ghar_Final_18122024.docx

## How long the subsidy takes to arrive
The official line is credit within 30 days of the commissioning paperwork being complete, but installer guides report 30 to 45 days, and 45 to 90 days from first registration to money received. Sources disagree, so never promise a customer a date. The subsidy goes to the customer's bank account, not to us, and it is never a payment towards our invoice.
Source: https://housing.com/news/how-to-apply-for-pm-surya-ghar-muft-bijli-yojana

## Loans for rooftop solar under the scheme
Households can get collateral-free, low-interest loans for systems up to 3 kW, at around 7%. The rate is pegged at about 0.5% above the repo rate, so it moves with the repo rate and differs by bank. Indian Bank caps these loans at Rs 2 lakh for systems up to 3 kW. Rates on bank pages are dated, so have the customer check the current rate with the bank. We do not arrange loans.
Source: https://indianbank.in/departments/ib-home-loan-new-roof-top-solar-scheme

## When the scheme ends
Reports describe the programme as running until March 2027, the end of financial year 2026-27. We found no confirmed extension as of November 2026. Do not tell customers it will or will not be extended. A customer who waits risks missing the scheme, which is a fair point to raise as long as you say the date comes from reports and not from us.
Source: https://www.pv-magazine-india.com/2026/08/31/india-adds-6-6-gw-of-rooftop-solar-in-h1-2026/
