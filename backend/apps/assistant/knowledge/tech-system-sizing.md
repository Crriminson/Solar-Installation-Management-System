## How many units a solar system generates per day
Sales material usually says 4 to 5 units per kW per day. State-wide figures range from about 1,150 kWh per kW a year in the North-East to about 1,800 kWh in Rajasthan, which is roughly 3.2 to 4.9 units per kW per day. So a 3 kW system makes about 10 to 15 units on an average day. Treat 4 units per kW per day as a planning figure, not a promise: it drops in monsoon and with shade or dirt. A generation estimate is advisory, not a guarantee.
Source: https://www.heavengreenenergy.com/blog/solar-generation-by-state

## What size of system does a customer need
Start from monthly units, not the bill amount. Size in kW = monthly units ÷ (30 × 4). Example: 300 units a month gives 300 ÷ 120 = 2.5 kW, so offer 3 kW if the roof allows. Check the sanctioned load, because the DISCOM limits the inverter to it, and MSEDCL has been limiting home systems based on average yearly usage. A capacity recommendation is advisory, and the approved survey decides what gets quoted.
Source: https://www.thehitavada.com/Encyc/2026/2/16/msedcl-limits-solar-capacity-for-domestic-sector-to-average-1-yr-power-consumption.html

## How much roof area is needed per kW
As a rule of thumb, allow about 8 to 10 square metres of shadow-free roof per kW, including spacing and walkways. Panels themselves take about 5 square metres per kW. This is a general figure, not from a checked source, so the survey's measured usable area decides. Keep clear of parapet shadows, water tanks, and access paths for cleaning.

## Best direction and tilt for panels in India
India is north of the equator, so panels should face south. The usual tilt is close to the site's latitude, for example about 19° in Mumbai and about 28° in Delhi. These are general rules, not from a checked source. Sheds use the roof's own slope. The MSEDCL specification says the angle follows site conditions for maximum sunlight and shadow-free generation, and may be reduced to fit more capacity if performance targets are still met.
Source: https://www.mahadiscom.in/ismart/media/Technical%20Spec.pdf

## Checking for shade on a roof
Shade costs far more output than its size suggests, because panels in a string share the same current. The MSEDCL specification asks for a shadow analysis using an instrument such as a Solar Pathfinder or software, and tells vendors to educate consumers to install only in shadow-free space. Check at the survey for water tanks, parapets, trees, neighbouring buildings and dish antennas, and remember the sun sits lower in winter, so shadows are longer than they look in summer.
Source: https://www.mahadiscom.in/ismart/media/Technical%20Spec.pdf

## How to size an off-grid battery
Battery capacity in kWh = load in kW × backup hours ÷ depth of discharge. Example: 1 kW of critical loads for 4 hours, with a depth of discharge of 0.8: 1 × 4 ÷ 0.8 = 5 kWh. A 150 Ah 12 V battery holds 150 × 12 ÷ 1000 = 1.8 kWh. Our package sets the depth of discharge, so use that figure. Always size from the load and hours on the survey, not from panel capacity.

## Panel performance warranty and degradation
Panels lose output slowly. The MSEDCL specification required performance above 97% in the first year, above 90% during the first 10 years, and above 80% during the next 15, with degradation below 1% a year, plus at least 5 years against manufacturing defects. Those are specification figures for that tender. Our own warranty policy and the maker's datasheet decide what a given customer has.
