## kW versus kWh, and what a unit is
kW is power, the rate of producing electricity. A system's size is in kW. kWh is energy, the amount produced over time, and one kWh is one unit on an electricity bill. A 3 kW system running at full power for 2 hours makes 6 kWh. kWp means kilowatt peak, the panel capacity under standard test conditions.

## What MPPT means
MPPT stands for Maximum Power Point Tracking. It is the circuit in an inverter or charge controller that keeps adjusting the voltage and current to get the most power from a string as sunlight and temperature change. Many inverters have two MPPT inputs, so two strings on different roof faces can each run at their best.

## Voc, Isc, Vmp and Imp on a panel datasheet
Voc is open-circuit voltage, the highest voltage a panel makes with nothing connected. Isc is short-circuit current, the highest current. Vmp and Imp are the voltage and current at maximum power, where the panel normally runs. Use Voc for the maximum voltage check, Vmp for the MPPT range and voltage drop, and Isc for current limits. STC means the standard test conditions these figures are measured at: 1000 W per square metre and 25°C cell temperature.

## DISCOM, MNRE, CFA and ALMM
DISCOM is the local electricity distribution company, such as MSEDCL in Maharashtra. MNRE is the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy. CFA is Central Financial Assistance, the formal name of the central subsidy. ALMM is the Approved List of Models and Manufacturers. MERC is Maharashtra's electricity regulator, and each state has its own commission.

## Net meter, net metering, gross metering and net billing
A net meter is a two-way meter that records units imported and exported. Under net metering, exported units are set against imported units. Under gross metering, all generation is sold to the DISCOM at a fixed rate. Under net billing, exports and imports are valued at separate rates. Virtual and group net metering share one plant's credits across several connections. The rules differ by state.

## ACDB, DCDB and junction box
The DCDB is the DC distribution box between the array and the inverter, holding the DC isolator, fuses and SPD. The ACDB is the AC distribution box between the inverter and the mains, holding the MCB and protection. A junction box or combiner joins strings at the array.

## Bypass diode and hot spot
A bypass diode inside the panel's junction box lets current go around a shaded or damaged section of cells. A hot spot is a cell that overheats because it is shaded or damaged and resists the current. Hot spots can crack the glass and ruin a panel, which is why even partial shade matters.

## Mono PERC, TOPCon, bifacial and half-cut
These are panel technologies. Mono PERC has been the mainstream type. TOPCon is a newer, slightly more efficient cell type. Bifacial panels also collect light on the rear side, which helps on a light-coloured roof with raised mounting. Half-cut panels use cells cut in two, which reduces losses and helps in partial shade. Use whatever our package specifies.

## Performance ratio
Performance ratio (PR) compares what a plant actually generates with what it could generate in theory at that site, as a percentage. It shows the combined effect of heat, dirt, shade, cable and inverter losses. Rooftop systems are often around 75% to 80%, as a general figure. A falling PR with no change in weather suggests something is wrong.

## Anti-islanding
Anti-islanding is the safety function that shuts an on-grid inverter down when the grid fails, so it does not energise lines that workers may be repairing. Inverters are tested for this under IEC 62116 or IS 16169. It is why on-grid systems give no backup during a power cut.
