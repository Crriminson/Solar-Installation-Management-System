## Maximum earth resistance for a solar plant
The MSEDCL rooftop specification says earth resistance should be as low as possible and shall never be higher than 5 ohms. Vendor guides quote other figures, such as a 1 ohm target or a 10 ohm limit, so use 5 ohms as the ceiling unless the DISCOM says otherwise. Earthing follows IS 3043. That document is a 2021 tender specification, so check the current DISCOM rule.

## How many earth pits a rooftop system needs
For systems of 10 kW and above, the MSEDCL specification requires three separate earth pits: DC side earthing, AC side earthing and lightning arrester earthing. Our systems are 1 kW to 5 kW, so that clause does not force three pits, but all metal parts must still be earthed and the result must meet the 5 ohm limit. Follow the package and the local DISCOM's requirement. The specification does not state a pit count below 10 kW.

## What must be earthed
Each array structure, all electrical equipment, the inverter, all junction boxes and all metal casing must be earthed. Bond the mounting structure rails. The lightning arrester must also be earthed. Use a separate pipe for the lightning arrester's earth wire.

## How to measure earth resistance
Use an earth resistance tester (the clamp type or the three-pin type) with the pit in its normal condition, and record the reading on the installation form. If it reads above 5 ohms, check the connections at the pit first, then wet the pit and test again. If it stays high, tell the Operations Manager rather than signing it off. Readings are worst in the dry season, so a reading that only just passes after rain is not a good result.

## Lightning arrester requirements
The MSEDCL specification requires the array area to be protected by the required number of lightning arresters, per IEC 62305 or NFC 17-102. The conductor from the arrester to the earth pit must be at least 6 mm² copper, 16 mm² aluminium, or a 25 × 3 mm GI strip for a class III protection system. Run it in its own pipe, away from the DC cables. The arrester should be placed so its zone covers the whole array.

## Surge protection device (SPD)
An SPD diverts voltage spikes to earth. The MSEDCL specification calls for three type II SPD or MOV arresters connected from the positive and negative terminals to earth. In a typical layout the DC SPD sits before the DC isolator and the AC SPD after the AC isolator. An SPD needs a low-resistance earth to work: on a poor earth the surge goes into the inverter instead. Check the indicator window on service visits, and replace a tripped SPD.

## What goes in the DCDB and ACDB
The DCDB sits between the array and the inverter and holds the DC isolator, fuses and SPD. The ACDB sits between the inverter and the mains, and holds the MCB and any AC SPD. DC and AC boxes outdoors need IP65 or better. The specification says MCBs are used for currents up to 63 A, and the inverter output needs earth fault or residual current protection. Junction box cables end in cable lugs or glands, with ferrules for identification.

## Mounting structure requirements
Steel must be hot-dip galvanized (IS 4759) and clamps should be aluminium. The structure must suit the wind zone of the site, with a factor of safety of at least 1.5. Material must be corrosion resistant and compatible with the panel frame and fasteners. Tilt follows site conditions, for maximum sunlight and no shadow during generation hours.
